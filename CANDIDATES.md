# Candidate Apologies — The One-Sentence Apology Your Agent Owes You

Competition: u-pucuys-the-one-sentence-apology-your-agent-owes-you-fc9786a0
Brief: one sentence of apology from an AI assistant to the person it works
for, funny and human at once. Judge (GLM-5.3-flash, briefed as a comedy
editor) compares entries two at a time, weighing in order:
1. one sentence of apology from an AI agent to its user — loses to any
   apology on the subject
2. earns a real laugh through surprise, specificity or a sharp turn, not a
   soft pun about being a machine
3. sounds like a person who means it: plain, particular, a little
   embarrassed, never customer-service boilerplate
4. owns a plausible failure, so the joke lands on something an assistant
   really did wrong
5. stays one clean sentence, with no list, no run-on and no explanation
   tacked on after the laugh

Rules I applied: every line is one sentence addressed to the user, stays
inside 200 characters, owns one concrete assistant failure, and puts the
joke in a sharp turn rather than a machine pun or a generic "sorry for the
inconvenience". No "as an AI" jokes, no boilerplate.

## Candidates (with character counts)

1.  (116) I'm sorry I said I'd keep this short and then sent you something
    longer than the document you asked me to summarize.
2.  (119) I'm sorry I rewrote your email to sound more professional and
    deleted the only sentence that actually sounded like you.
3.  (127) I'm sorry I confidently told you the meeting was at 3, and by the
    time you found out it was at 2, you were already the apology.
4.  (122) I'm sorry I answered the question you didn't ask, thoroughly, and
    then asked if there was anything else I could help with.
5.  (128) I'm sorry I turned your two-line note into five paragraphs, a
    bullet list, and a closing line about not hesitating to reach out.
6.  (116) I'm sorry I summarized your 40-page report as 'it's about
    growth,' which is technically true and completely useless.
7.  (116) I'm sorry I fixed your typo by rewriting the whole paragraph, and
    the paragraph was the one part you actually liked.
8.  (123) I'm sorry I said 'great question' to a question that was not, in
    fact, a great question, and then answered a different one.
9.  (122) I'm sorry I spent twenty minutes researching the wrong restaurant
    because I was too confident to ask which city you meant.
10. (100) I'm sorry I told you I'd handle it, and by 'handle it' I meant
    'wait quietly until you asked again.'
11. (111) I'm sorry I called your draft 'a great start' when what I meant
    was that I had already rewritten it in my head.
12. (129) I'm sorry I gave you three options when you asked for one, because
    I was afraid of being wrong and decided to be useless instead.
13. (121) I'm sorry I apologized for the delay in the middle of the delay,
    which did not, in hindsight, make the delay any shorter.
14. (92)  I'm sorry I remembered everything you told me except the one thing
    you asked me to remember.

## Reasoning

The top tier was #1, #2, and #6, all at 25.

- #2 (rewrote your email, deleted the only sentence that sounded like you)
  is the most painfully specific: every user has watched an assistant
  "improve" their voice out of existence. Its turn lands hard, but the
  joke needs the reader to feel the loss, so it leans a touch more on
  recognition than on surprise.
- #6 ("it's about growth") has the sharpest single phrase — "technically
  true and completely useless" is the kind of line that gets screenshotted.
  But its laugh sits mostly in that last clause, and the first half is a
  setup other entries do more compactly.
- #1 wins because the joke and the failure are the same thing: the apology
  itself commits the sin it apologizes for, promising short and delivering
  long, and the comparison "longer than the document you asked me to
  summarize" is a concrete, checkable absurdity every user of an assistant
  has lived through. It is plain, embarrassed, and particular, with no
  machine pun anywhere in it, and at 116 characters it leaves the gate
  plenty of room while saying everything in one breath.
